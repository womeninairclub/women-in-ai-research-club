How It Works

Stage 01

Screening Round — Pitch the idea.

The screening round is about understanding the problem and presenting a feasible plan — not submitting a finished project.

What teams submit

  1. Team name and members
  2. Selected track and challenge
  3. Problem understanding
  4. Proposed solution
  5. Key innovation
  6. Technical approach
  7. Dataset / resource plan
  8. Expected output
  9. Team member roles
  10. Final-round implementation plan

Screening evaluation

Problem understanding 20%
Solution feasibility 20%
Technical approach 20%
Innovation / originality 15%
Usefulness / potential impact 15%
Clarity of pitch 10%
Important: Screening is not a race to produce the most code before selection. Teams are assessed on their understanding, feasibility, technical reasoning and proposed execution.

Final Submission

What your GitHub submission should contain.

The website hosts the challenge and rules. The final project itself is submitted through the official GitHub workflow.

✓ Project title & selected challenge
✓ Team members & contributions
✓ Problem understanding
✓ Solution / methodology
✓ Source code
✓ Requirements / setup
✓ Evaluation & results
✓ Demo / screenshots / video where required
✓ Limitations & responsible-AI note
✓ AI-tools disclosure
GitHub rule: The final repository must be accessible to the judging team at the time of submission. Teams are responsible for checking that links, files and instructions work before the deadline.

Rules & Regulations

Fair participation. Clear expectations.

Eligibility

  • Open to participants internationally.
  • Participation is free.
  • Teams must follow the official registration requirements.

Team Participation

  • Each participant should be part of only one team.
  • Team information must remain accurate throughout the event.
  • Team members must be identified in the official submission.

Challenge Selection

  • Teams select one official challenge.
  • All participants receive access to the published challenge set at the same release point.
  • Solutions should address the selected challenge.

Pre-event Work

  • Teams may study the released problem, research resources and plan their approach.
  • Substantially completed final solutions should not be developed before the official final build period.

Data & Resources

  • Use public, permitted or explicitly provided resources.
  • Respect licenses, attribution, access restrictions and privacy requirements.
  • Do not expose private or identifiable data.

AI Assistance

  • Teams must disclose significant use of AI coding, generation or research tools.
  • Teams remain responsible for understanding and verifying submitted work.
  • AI-generated content must not be presented as independently verified fact.

Security & Safety

  • No unauthorized access, attacks against real systems, credential theft or malicious deployment.
  • Security testing must remain within permitted datasets, sandboxed environments or the team's own systems.

Academic Integrity

  • No plagiarism or misrepresentation of another team's work.
  • External code, datasets and resources must be acknowledged where required.

Medical / High-impact AI

  • Healthcare challenges are research/educational prototypes.
  • Do not claim clinical diagnosis or treatment capability.
  • Document limitations, uncertainty and dataset constraints.

Submission Deadline

  • Final submission closes at 6:00 PM on the main event day.
  • Teams should verify repository access before submission.
  • Late submissions may be excluded according to the final event rules.

Code of Conduct

  • Respect participants, judges, organizers and collaborators.
  • No harassment, discrimination, abusive behavior or disruption of the event.

Organizer Decision

  • The organizing team may clarify rules when necessary.
  • Final decisions regarding eligibility, screening and event administration will follow the published rules.
Final Evaluation

What judges will look for.

Judging should focus on the stated challenge requirements, the quality of implementation and the team's ability to demonstrate and explain its solution.

Problem understanding Does the solution clearly address the selected challenge?
Technical implementation Is the approach appropriate, functional and reproducible?
Solution quality How well does the prototype perform against its stated objective?
Innovation Does the team bring a thoughtful or useful approach beyond the baseline?
Usability & demonstration Can users and judges understand and interact with the solution?
Responsible AI Are limitations, privacy, fairness, safety and uncertainty addressed where relevant?

Optional extensions are not mandatory requirements. A team's score should be grounded in the published challenge requirements and the quality of what it actually demonstrates.

FAQ

Questions participants may have.

When will the problem statements be released?

They will be published on the official hackathon website two days before the main event at the announced release time.

Can we choose any challenge?

Teams select one of the officially published challenges for their screening proposal.

Is the screening round the final competition?

No. Screening is used to select teams for the main final round.

Do we need a finished project for screening?

No. The screening stage focuses on your understanding of the challenge and what you propose to build.

Where do we submit the final project?

Selected teams will submit their final project through the official GitHub-based submission workflow described on this website.

Can we use AI tools?

AI tools may be used subject to the final event rules. Significant use must be disclosed, and teams remain responsible for their work.

Can we use public datasets?

Yes, where their license, access terms and intended use permit it. Teams must document the source and comply with applicable requirements.

Are optional features required?

No. Optional extensions are intended for teams that want to extend their prototype; they are not mandatory prerequisites.
